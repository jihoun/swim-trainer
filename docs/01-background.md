# Background: ZWIM and why we build our own

## The reference product

**ZWIM** (zwim.com) is a "connected swim training" system from the team behind
**ZEN8**, a dryland swim trainer sold since 2020 (20,000+ users claimed).
Founders Victor Jarland and Charlotte, based in Lisbon.

The ZWIM Smart Trainer is:

- **ZWIM Bench**: an inflatable box, **35 × 50 × 80 cm** (H × W × L), built-in
  speed pump, 4 kg, packs into a backpack.
- **Three "Propulsion Bands"** (LVL 1–3) anchored to a door, window or handle
  at roughly 90–130 cm, depending on the athlete's height.
- **ZWIM Smart Paddles** (patent pending) measuring force and rhythm of the
  pull and reporting **"Propulsive Watts"**, plus stroke rate, left/right
  balance and efficiency.
- An app with a game, structured workouts, free sessions and challenges;
  15–35 minute sessions between pool days.

Pricing at pre-order (Oct 2026): £349 trainer + £99/year membership.
Shipping only to the **UK and US** (Europe next), batch 2 shipping Dec 2026.

## Why DIY

- It doesn't ship to Korea.
- The value is in two things that are easy to reproduce: a **horizontal body
  position** on a bench and **swim-specific band work**. The data is the
  differentiator, and a band + load cell gives us the data.
- Publishing the result as a standard **BLE Cycling Power** sensor means every
  existing tool (Garmin, Zwift, TrainingPeaks) works on day one, with no app to
  build.

## Don't confuse with

"Zwim" (2017) was a different product: heads-up-display swim goggles by a
Korean team (Taegoo Kang), crowdfunded on Indiegogo. Unrelated.

## Evidence that dryland band work helps

Swim benches and stretch cords have been used by swim programs for decades.
Studies cited by ZWIM: Sadowski et al. 2020, Pinos et al. 2021, Naczk et al.
2017. Bench research also shows a fixed bench limits body roll compared with
real freestyle, which is one reason a narrow bench is acceptable, even good.
Treat it as a **supplement** to pool sessions, not a replacement.
